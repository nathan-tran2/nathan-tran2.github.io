Overview

Autoestima is Spanish for “self-esteem,” read here as auto (car) plus estima (estimate). It started as our CSC 422 project on predicting used-car prices and became a finished web app.

Take a photo of a car, confirm what it is, add its year, mileage, condition, title and crash history, and you get an estimated value with a likely range, its value over time from new to five years out, the details that pushed the price up or down, and a deal grade if you're looking at a listing.

Browsing works like Pinterest or Depop: a photo-first For You feed that learns what you like, Shop and Best deals to search and filter, and a Sell page where anyone signed in can post a car.

How it works

  1. Photo to carYOLO finds the vehicle and crops it, then OpenCLIP compares the crop with about 350 model families from our data. The user confirms the guess and always picks the year, since a photo can’t show it.
  2. Only real trimsThe Year, Make, Model and Trim menus only offer what was actually sold that year, from the EPA’s fueleconomy.gov list, so there’s no 2025 Camry TRD.
  3. Details to valueA CatBoost model trained on Craigslist listings predicts the price from make, model, age, mileage, condition, title status and more. The range covers 80% of real prices for similar cars.
  4. Value over timeThe same model runs at every age from new to five years out, adding the owner’s own miles per year, and the curve is smoothed so value never rises with age.
  5. Why this priceSHAP values show how much each detail moved this car’s estimate compared with an average car.
  6. Deals and a feed that learnsEach listing’s asking price is compared with the estimate: Great deal, Good deal, Fair price or Overpriced. The For You feed ranks cars against a taste profile built from a style quiz and what you view, save and value.

Results

15.1%median error on cars the model never saw
$3,095average error (MAE)
0.80R² on log price
20,957held-out test cars

Measured on 20,957 held-out cars. The backend has 55 automated tests covering the estimates, deal grades, the feed and the photo pipeline.

The prices come from 2021 Craigslist listings, when used-car prices ran unusually high, so the app presents estimates as relative values rather than today’s exact market price.

The model work

Before the app, our team trained and compared CatBoost, Random Forest and XGBoost, scored on MAE, RMSE and R². I owned feature engineering, evaluation and explainability: deriving vehicle age and average miles per year, analysing residuals to find where the models broke down, and using SHAP to explain what drives any single prediction. I also led the computer vision extension that identifies a car from a photo and feeds it into the pricing pipeline.
